## Relevant Venues for the Aspiring Tech-Law Scholar

### Conferences

Conferences are a great way to network and practice speaking skills, but beware that the scope of conference papers are limited, so decide on the trajectory of the paper early. 

You also need to decide whether the conference publication is [archival](https://academia.stackexchange.com/questions/138797/what-exactly-is-a-non-archival-venue-and-workshop-with-proceedings) or not. 
In other words, is this a work that fits into a bigger journal article? In which case, pick a non-archival route like workshop, poster, or work-in-progress -- these are generally less competitive.
Otherwise, you will be gunning for the archival publication with most other applicants. If there is interest, I will file some resources for dealing with conference reviews here.

Here are some tech-law conferences, primarily ACM.
* [ACM Conference on Fairness, Accountability, and Transparency](https://facctconference.org/): January CFPs, workshop and other non-archival publication options always available
* [ACM Symposium on Computer Science and Law](https://computersciencelaw.org/): September CFPs workshop and other non-archival publication options available sometimes
* [ACM Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI)](https://chi2027.acm.org/): September CFPs, workshop and other non-archival publication options always available. Highly competitive.
* [WeRobot](https://www.werobot2026.eu/): CFPs generally in the fall, with some workshop and other non-archival publication options. Archival publication opportunities vary from year to year.

As a practical consideration, do note that ACM conferences generally cost several hundred American dollars to attend, and at least one author must attend. 
Some ACM conferences provide travel fundings and/or waive registration fees for volunteers, but volunteering positions are both often random and competitive so they're not a sure bet.
